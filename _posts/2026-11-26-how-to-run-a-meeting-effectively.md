# How to run a meeting effectively

Your manager walks over and says: “I need you to run a meeting.”<br>
What the hell does that mean?

Running an effective meeting is a skill that few people are explicitly taught.

Here's a 6-step framework to help you impress your manager and level up your career.

**Step 0: Prepare before the meeting.**

Even 15 minutes of preparation can go a long way.

You don't need to have all the answers, especially if the purpose of the meeting is to gather information.

But you should know what you want to get out of it.

**Step 1: Break the ice.**

Small talk is more important than you think.

It helps people get comfortable and encourages them to speak up later in the meeting.

1–3 minutes is enough.

Ask something simple:

“What food are you looking forward to during the holidays?”

Or ask about their plans for an upcoming weekend or event.

**Step 2: Start with the big picture.**

Don't assume everyone knows what you're working on.

People are switching between meetings, projects and priorities throughout the day.

In 1–2 sentences, explain what you're trying to achieve and what it's for, using simple, high-level language.

**Step 3: Go a step deeper.**

Connect the big picture to the specific problem you're trying to solve.

Explain why you need their input.

Then end with a question.

Give people an opportunity to contribute.

**Step 4: Ask clarifying questions.**

Be curious about what others think.

Use open-ended “what” and “how” questions.

- What challenges do you see with this approach?
- How could we make this work better?
- What are we missing?

These questions encourage discussion without sounding accusatory.

**Step 5: Conclude with next actions.**

Who will do what, and by when?

Never skip this step.

Otherwise, people may leave the meeting wondering what was actually accomplished.

Running a meeting isn't about having all the answers.<br>
It's about creating the conditions for people to find answers together.<br>
That's a leadership skill worth developing.
